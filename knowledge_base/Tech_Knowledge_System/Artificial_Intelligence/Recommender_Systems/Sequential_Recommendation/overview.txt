[CHUNK: DEFINITION]
Sequential recommendation systems predict user preferences by analyzing the chronological order of past interactions. They capture dynamic user behavior and item transitions, focusing on the sequence rather than just aggregated historical data. This approach is crucial for understanding evolving tastes and context-dependent choices.

[CHUNK: CORE_CONCEPTS]
- Session-based Recommendation: Predicting the next item within a short, often anonymous, user session.
- Next-item Prediction: Forecasting the immediate subsequent item a user will interact with based on their recent history.
- Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs): Neural architectures like GRU4Rec designed to process sequential data and capture temporal dependencies.
- Transformers: Attention-based models (e.g., SASRec, BERT4Rec) that effectively model long-range dependencies in user interaction sequences.
- Markov Chains: Probabilistic models (e.g., FPMC) that predict the next item based on the current or a few preceding items.
- Context-Aware Recommendation: Incorporating additional contextual information (time, location, device) into sequential models.
- Cold Start Problem: Challenges in recommending to new users or with new items due to insufficient interaction data.

[CHUNK: SYSTEM_DESIGN_PERSPECTIVE]
Sequential recommenders often operate on real-time interaction streams, requiring low-latency inference and dynamic model updates. They typically involve feature engineering for sequence representation, model training on historical sequences, and online serving infrastructure. Scalability is a key concern due to the high dimensionality and volume of sequential data.
