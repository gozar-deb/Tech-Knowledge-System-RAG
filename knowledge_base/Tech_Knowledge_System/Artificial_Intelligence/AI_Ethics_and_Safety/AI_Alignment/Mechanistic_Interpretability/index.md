## Quick Definition
Mechanistic interpretability is a field dedicated to reverse-engineering the internal computations of neural networks to understand precisely how they arrive at their decisions. It involves dissecting models into their fundamental components like circuits and features to uncover the algorithms learned during training.

## Key Concepts
- Features → Specific patterns or properties detected by model components.
- Circuits → Subgraphs of a neural network responsible for particular computations.
- Superposition → Multiple features encoded by fewer neurons than their theoretical capacity.
- Polysemanticity → A single neuron responding to diverse, seemingly unrelated inputs.
- Induction Heads → Transformer circuits that facilitate in-context learning by identifying and replicating patterns.
- Causal Tracing → A method to map information flow and causal relationships within a neural network.
- Activation Patching → A technique to test hypotheses about circuit function by modifying internal activations.
