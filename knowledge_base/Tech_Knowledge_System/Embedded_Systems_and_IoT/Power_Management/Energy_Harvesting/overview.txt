[CHUNK: DEFINITION]
Energy harvesting is the process of capturing and converting ambient energy from the environment into usable electrical power. This technique enables self-powered electronic devices, reducing reliance on traditional batteries and extending operational lifespan. It is also known as power harvesting, energy scavenging, or ambient power.

[CHUNK: CORE_CONCEPTS]
- Transduction: The conversion of one form of energy (e.g., kinetic, thermal, solar) into electrical energy.
- Ambient Energy Sources: Environmental energy forms like light, heat, motion, radio waves, and vibrations.
- Low Power Electronics: Devices designed to operate with minimal power consumption, crucial for energy harvesting viability.
- Power Management Units (PMUs): Circuits that manage the harvested energy, including storage, regulation, and delivery to the load.
- Energy Storage: Components like supercapacitors or rechargeable batteries used to store harvested energy for intermittent use.
- Self-Powered Devices: Electronic systems that operate autonomously using only harvested energy.
- Hybrid Harvesting: Combining multiple energy sources or transduction methods to improve power output and reliability.

[CHUNK: SYSTEM_DESIGN_PERSPECTIVE]
Energy harvesting systems integrate transducers, power management circuits, and energy storage to provide autonomous power. Design involves selecting appropriate harvesting mechanisms based on available ambient energy, optimizing conversion efficiency, and ensuring stable power delivery to the load. It's critical for remote or maintenance-free IoT deployments.

[CHUNK: TOOLS_AND_TECH]
Categorized tools:
- Transducers: Piezoelectric materials, thermoelectric generators (TEGs), photovoltaic cells, RF rectifiers, electromagnetic coils.
- ICs: Ultra-low power microcontrollers, dedicated energy harvesting PMUs (e.g., from Analog Devices, Texas Instruments).
- Storage: Thin-film batteries, supercapacitors, solid-state batteries.
- Simulation: SPICE, MATLAB/Simulink for modeling energy conversion and power management.

[CHUNK: REAL_WORLD_USE]
Wireless sensor nodes in remote industrial monitoring, smart home devices powered by indoor light, wearable electronics charged by body heat or motion, and self-powered medical implants. It also extends to infrastructure monitoring where battery replacement is impractical or costly.

[CHUNK: FAILURE_MODES]
Insufficient ambient energy leading to power starvation, degradation of harvesting materials over time, inefficient energy conversion, storage element failure (e.g., battery cycle life), and environmental factors impacting transducer performance (e.g., dust on solar cells).

[CHUNK: OPTIMIZATION_STRATEGIES]
Maximum Power Point Tracking (MPPT) for solar and thermal harvesters, impedance matching between transducer and load, efficient power management algorithms, hybrid harvesting from multiple sources, and duty cycling of the load to minimize power consumption.

[CHUNK: SECURITY_IMPLICATIONS]
While not directly a security vulnerability, energy harvesting systems can be susceptible to physical tampering or environmental manipulation to disrupt power supply. Ensuring robust physical security and tamper detection for critical devices is important. → See: Tech_Knowledge_System/Cybersecurity/Physical_Security
