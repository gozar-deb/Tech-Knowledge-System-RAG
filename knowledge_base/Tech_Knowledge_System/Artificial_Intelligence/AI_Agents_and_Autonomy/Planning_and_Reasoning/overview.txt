[CHUNK: DEFINITION]
Planning and Reasoning in AI involves developing computational methods for intelligent agents to autonomously determine sequences of actions to achieve goals, infer new knowledge from existing facts, and make informed decisions. It encompasses symbolic, probabilistic, and learning-based approaches to navigate complex environments and solve problems.

[CHUNK: CORE_CONCEPTS]
- Planning: The process of finding a sequence of actions that transforms an initial state into a goal state.
- Reasoning: The ability to draw conclusions and make inferences from available information, often using logic or probability.
- State-Space Search: Exploring possible states and transitions to find a path to a goal, fundamental to many planning algorithms.
- Knowledge Representation: Formalizing information about the world, actions, and goals in a way that AI systems can process.
- Action Selection: Choosing the most appropriate action at any given moment based on current state, goals, and learned policies.
- Goal-Oriented Behavior: Designing agents that act purposefully to achieve predefined objectives, often involving complex planning.
- Heuristics: Rules of thumb or educated guesses used to guide search algorithms and improve efficiency in planning.
- Logical Inference: Deriving new facts or conclusions from a set of premises using rules of logic.

[CHUNK: SYSTEM_DESIGN_PERSPECTIVE]
In system design, planning and reasoning modules are critical for creating adaptive and autonomous AI. They often operate within a perception-cognition-action loop, translating high-level goals into executable steps and adapting to dynamic environments. Robust knowledge representation is key for effective reasoning.

[CHUNK: TOOLS_AND_TECH]
Categorized tools:
- Languages: Python, Prolog, Lisp, Java
- Frameworks: PDDL (Planning Domain Definition Language), PyTorch (for RL-based planning), TensorFlow, Golog
- Infrastructure: Automated theorem provers, SAT solvers, constraint programming toolkits

[CHUNK: REAL_WORLD_USE]
Planning and reasoning are implemented in autonomous robotics for navigation and task execution, such as Mars rovers scheduling scientific observations. They are also vital in logistics for optimizing delivery routes and resource allocation, and in game AI for strategic decision-making.

[CHUNK: FAILURE_MODES]
Common failure modes include incomplete or incorrect world models leading to suboptimal plans, computational intractability for complex problems, and brittleness to unexpected environmental changes. Over-reliance on static knowledge can hinder adaptation, and poor heuristic design can lead to inefficient search.

[CHUNK: OPTIMIZATION_STRATEGIES]
Optimization involves using hierarchical planning to decompose large problems, employing advanced search algorithms like A* with informed heuristics, and integrating machine learning for adaptive policy learning. Parallelization of search and distributed reasoning can also enhance performance.

[CHUNK: SECURITY_IMPLICATIONS]
Security implications arise from potential adversarial manipulation of an agent's knowledge base or goal definitions, leading to malicious planning. Robustness against false information and secure reasoning processes are crucial to prevent exploitation and ensure safe autonomous operation.

Advanced topics include explainable AI planning, where agents can justify their decisions, and multi-agent planning, which addresses coordination and cooperation among multiple intelligent entities. Hybrid approaches combining symbolic and neural methods are also a key research area.
