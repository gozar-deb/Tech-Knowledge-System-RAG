[CHUNK: DEFINITION]
Reentrancy attacks exploit a smart contract's vulnerability when an external call is made to another contract before the state of the calling contract is updated. Flash loan attacks leverage uncollateralized loans, executed and repaid within a single transaction, to manipulate market prices or exploit other protocol vulnerabilities for profit.

[CHUNK: CORE_CONCEPTS]
- Reentrancy: A vulnerability where an external call allows the external contract to call back into the original contract before its state is updated.
- Flash Loan: An uncollateralized loan that must be borrowed and repaid within the same blockchain transaction.
- External Call: A message call from one smart contract to another, or to a regular address.
- State Manipulation: Altering the internal variables or balances of a smart contract in an unintended way.
- The DAO Hack: A prominent reentrancy attack in 2016 that resulted in the theft of millions of Ether.
- DeFi Exploits: Attacks targeting decentralized finance protocols, often involving flash loans to manipulate asset prices.

[CHUNK: SYSTEM_DESIGN_PERSPECTIVE]
These attacks highlight critical design flaws in smart contract interactions, particularly concerning external calls and state management. Robust systems must implement checks-effects-interactions patterns and reentrancy guards to prevent unauthorized re-execution. Flash loan attacks necessitate careful oracle design and liquidity pool management to mitigate price manipulation risks.

[CHUNK: REAL_WORLD_USE]
Reentrancy was famously exploited in The DAO hack, leading to a hard fork of the Ethereum blockchain. Flash loan attacks have been used to drain liquidity from various DeFi protocols, such as the bZx and PancakeSwap exploits, by manipulating token prices on decentralized exchanges.

[CHUNK: FAILURE_MODES]
Reentrancy failures occur when a contract's state is not updated before an external call, allowing repeated withdrawals or actions. Flash loan failures arise from oracle manipulation, insufficient liquidity, or logic errors in price calculation within DeFi protocols, leading to economic exploits.

[CHUNK: OPTIMIZATION_STRATEGIES]
To mitigate reentrancy, use the checks-effects-interactions pattern, reentrancy guards (e.g., mutex locks), and limit external calls. For flash loans, implement robust price oracles, ensure deep liquidity, and conduct thorough economic security audits to prevent manipulation and arbitrage opportunities.

[CHUNK: SECURITY_IMPLICATIONS]
Reentrancy creates a critical attack surface for unauthorized fund withdrawals and state corruption. Flash loans, while legitimate DeFi primitives, can be weaponized to execute complex economic exploits, manipulate markets, and drain protocol funds, posing significant systemic risk to the DeFi ecosystem.
