## Quick Definition
Platform liability refers to the legal responsibility of online platforms for content or activities conducted by their users. This complex area involves balancing free speech, platform moderation, and protecting users from harm, often varying significantly across jurisdictions. It's a critical aspect of tech regulation impacting social media, e-commerce, and content hosting services.

## Key Concepts
- **Section 230 (CDA)**: US law protecting platforms from liability for third-party content and good-faith moderation.
- **Digital Services Act (DSA)**: EU regulation imposing obligations on online platforms regarding illegal content, transparency, and user protection.
- **Intermediary Liability**: General legal principle concerning the responsibility of entities that facilitate communication or transactions between third parties.
- **Notice and Takedown**: System where platforms remove illegal content upon notification, often a condition for liability protection.
- **Safe Harbor Provisions**: Legal clauses that shield platforms from certain liabilities under specific conditions.
- **Content Moderation**: Processes and policies platforms use to monitor and control user-generated content.
- **Freedom of Speech**: Constitutional or legal right to express opinions and ideas without government restriction, often clashing with platform liability.
- **Harmful Content**: Categories of content (e.g., hate speech, misinformation, illegal goods) that platforms are pressured to address.
