[CHUNK: DEFINITION]
Typography is the art and technique of arranging type to make written language legible, readable, and appealing when displayed. Color theory is a body of practical guidance to color mixing and the visual effects of a specific color combination. Both are fundamental to visual design, influencing user perception and interaction.

[CHUNK: CORE_CONCEPTS]
- Legibility: The ease with which individual characters or words can be distinguished.
- Readability: The ease with which text can be read and understood.
- Typeface vs. Font: Typeface is the design; font is the specific style and size.
- Color Wheel: A circular chart showing the relationships between colors.
- Color Harmony: The arrangement of colors in a visually pleasing way.
- Contrast: The difference in luminance or color that makes an object distinguishable.
- Hue: The pure spectrum color (e.g., red, blue, green).
- Saturation: The intensity or purity of a color.
- Value/Brightness: The lightness or darkness of a color.

[CHUNK: SYSTEM_DESIGN_PERSPECTIVE]
In system design, typography and color theory are critical for establishing visual hierarchy, brand identity, and user guidance. Consistent application ensures a cohesive user interface, reducing cognitive load and enhancing usability. Design systems often codify these elements to maintain uniformity across complex applications.

[CHUNK: REAL_WORLD_USE]
Google's Material Design system meticulously defines typography scales and color palettes to ensure consistent branding and accessibility across its vast ecosystem of applications. Apple's Human Interface Guidelines provide strict rules for font usage (San Francisco) and color semantics to create a unified and intuitive user experience on iOS and macOS.

[CHUNK: FAILURE_MODES]
Poor legibility due to insufficient contrast or inappropriate font choices leads to user frustration and accessibility barriers. Inconsistent color usage can confuse users about interactive elements or brand identity. Overuse of decorative fonts or clashing color schemes can make an interface appear unprofessional and difficult to navigate.

[CHUNK: OPTIMIZATION_STRATEGIES]
Optimize typography by selecting typefaces with good legibility, establishing a clear typographic hierarchy, and ensuring adequate line height and letter spacing. Optimize color by adhering to accessibility guidelines for contrast, using a limited and harmonious color palette, and employing color semantically to convey meaning (e.g., red for error, green for success).

[CHUNK: SECURITY_IMPLICATIONS]
While not directly a security domain, poor typography and color choices can indirectly impact security. Illegible security warnings or misleading color cues (e.g., green for a dangerous action) can lead to user errors, making phishing attempts harder to detect or critical information overlooked. Consistent, clear visual communication is key.
