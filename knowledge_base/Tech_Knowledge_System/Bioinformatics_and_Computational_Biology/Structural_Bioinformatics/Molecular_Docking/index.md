## Quick Definition
Molecular docking is a computational technique used to predict the binding orientation and affinity of a small molecule (ligand) to a macromolecule (receptor), typically a protein. It's a fundamental tool in structure-based drug design, helping to identify potential drug candidates and understand molecular interactions.

## Key Concepts
- Ligand → A molecule that binds to a receptor.
- Receptor → A macromolecule, often a protein, that a ligand binds to.
- Binding Affinity → The strength of the interaction between a ligand and its receptor.
- Scoring Function → An algorithm that estimates the binding affinity and quality of a ligand-receptor pose.
- Docking Pose → The predicted 3D position and orientation of a ligand within a receptor's binding site.
- Virtual Screening → A computational method to search large libraries of small molecules for those that are most likely to bind to a drug target.
