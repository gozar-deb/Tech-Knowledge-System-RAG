## Quick Definition
Audio Language Models (ALMs) are AI systems that integrate audio and text processing, enabling them to understand, reason about, and generate audio-centric content. They leverage large datasets of paired audio and text to perform tasks like speech recognition, audio synthesis, and multimodal comprehension, bridging the gap between acoustic signals and linguistic meaning.

## Key Concepts
- Multimodal Learning → Combining different data types (e.g., audio, text) for comprehensive AI understanding.
- Audio-Text Data → Datasets linking audio recordings with their textual descriptions for model training.
- Audio Tokenization → Converting continuous audio signals into discrete units for language model processing.
- Generative Audio → AI's ability to create new audio content, including speech, music, and sound effects.
- Cross-Modal Alignment → Techniques to match representations from different modalities in a shared space.
- Object Hallucination → A common failure where ALMs generate non-existent audio events or attributes.
- Unified Audio Language Model → A single model capable of processing and generating diverse audio types (speech, music, sound).
- Self-Supervised Pre-training → Learning from unlabeled audio data by predicting hidden parts of the input.
