## Quick Definition
Decentralized Finance (DeFi) refers to an ecosystem of financial applications built on blockchain technology, primarily Ethereum, that aims to disintermediate traditional financial services. It enables peer-to-peer transactions and financial activities like lending, borrowing, and trading without the need for central authorities like banks or brokers.

## Key Concepts
- **Decentralization** → Control and decision-making are distributed among network participants rather than a central entity.
- **Smart Contracts** → Self-executing agreements with the terms directly written into code, automating transactions and processes.
- **dApps (Decentralized Applications)** → Applications built on blockchain networks that operate without central control, powered by smart contracts.
- **Blockchain** → A distributed, immutable ledger technology that underpins DeFi, ensuring transparency and security.
- **Cryptocurrency** → Digital assets used within DeFi for transactions, collateral, and governance.
- **Lending and Borrowing** → Users can lend their crypto assets to earn interest or borrow assets by providing collateral, all facilitated by smart contracts.
- **Decentralized Exchanges (DEXs)** → Platforms that allow users to trade cryptocurrencies directly with each other without an intermediary.
- **Yield Farming** → The practice of staking or lending crypto assets in various DeFi protocols to generate high returns or rewards.
- **Liquidity Pools** → Crowdsourced pools of tokens locked in smart contracts that facilitate trading and other DeFi functions.
- **Staking** → Locking up cryptocurrency to support the operations of a blockchain network and earn rewards.
