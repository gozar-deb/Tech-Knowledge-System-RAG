## Quick Definition
Quantum entanglement describes a profound connection between particles, where their quantum states are linked irrespective of spatial separation. Bell states are a set of four maximally entangled two-qubit states, serving as a cornerstone for understanding and applying entanglement in quantum information science.

## Key Concepts
- Quantum Entanglement → A phenomenon where particles become interconnected, sharing a unified quantum state.
- Bell States → Specific maximally entangled states of two qubits, crucial for quantum communication and computation.
- Superposition → The ability of a quantum system to exist in multiple states simultaneously.
- Non-locality → The property of entangled particles to exhibit correlations that transcend classical spatial limitations.
- Decoherence → The loss of quantum coherence due to interaction with the environment, destroying entanglement.
- Quantum Teleportation → The transfer of a quantum state from one location to another using entanglement.
- Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) → Cryptographic protocols leveraging entanglement for secure communication.
