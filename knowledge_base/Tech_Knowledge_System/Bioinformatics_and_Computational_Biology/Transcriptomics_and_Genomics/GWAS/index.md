## Quick Definition
Genome-Wide Association Studies (GWAS) are an observational study design used in genetics to identify genetic variants, typically SNPs, that are associated with a particular disease or trait by scanning the entire genome of many individuals.

## Key Concepts
- SNP → A single nucleotide polymorphism, a variation at a single base pair.
- Allele Frequency → The proportion of a specific allele in a population.
- Manhattan Plot → A plot visualizing GWAS results across the genome.
- P-value → Statistical measure of evidence against a null hypothesis.
- Linkage Disequilibrium → Non-random association of alleles at different loci.
- Imputation → Inferring ungenotyped SNPs using reference panels.
