[CHUNK: DEFINITION]
Power management in embedded systems and IoT involves optimizing energy consumption to extend battery life, reduce heat, and ensure reliable operation. It encompasses techniques from hardware design to software algorithms to minimize power draw while meeting performance requirements.

[CHUNK: CORE_CONCEPTS]
- Low-Power Modes: States like sleep, deep sleep, and hibernate that reduce power consumption by disabling non-essential components.
- Dynamic Voltage and Frequency Scaling (DVFS): Adjusting operating voltage and clock frequency based on workload to optimize power.
- Power Gating: Completely shutting off power to inactive circuit blocks to eliminate leakage current.
- Energy Harvesting: Capturing ambient energy (solar, thermal, kinetic) to power devices, reducing reliance on batteries.
- Battery Management Systems (BMS): Circuits and algorithms for monitoring, protecting, and optimizing battery performance and lifespan.
- Quiescent Current (Iq): The current drawn by a voltage regulator when no load is applied, critical for always-on low-power devices.
- Power Budgeting: Allocating and managing power consumption across different components to stay within design limits.
- Wake-up Mechanisms: Events or timers that transition a system from a low-power state back to active operation.

[CHUNK: SYSTEM_DESIGN_PERSPECTIVE]
Effective power management is integral to system architecture, influencing component selection, board layout, and software scheduling. [CHUNK: REAL_WORLD_USE]
Wireless sensor nodes in remote environmental monitoring systems utilize deep sleep modes and energy harvesting to operate autonomously for years. Smart home devices employ DVFS and power gating to maintain responsiveness while minimizing idle power consumption. Wearable health trackers rely on sophisticated BMS for extended battery life and safety.

[CHUNK: FAILURE_MODES]
Inadequate power budgeting can lead to premature battery depletion or system instability under peak loads. Improper low-power mode transitions can result in data loss or missed events. Overheating due to inefficient power conversion or insufficient thermal management can cause component damage. Faulty BMS can lead to battery overcharge/discharge.

[CHUNK: OPTIMIZATION_STRATEGIES]
Implement fine-grained power control at the component level, utilizing low-power peripherals and efficient data transfer protocols. Optimize software algorithms to minimize active time and maximize sleep duration. Employ dynamic power scaling techniques based on real-time workload. Leverage energy harvesting where feasible to supplement power.

[CHUNK: SECURITY_IMPLICATIONS]
Power side-channel attacks can exploit power consumption patterns to infer cryptographic keys or sensitive data. Denial-of-service attacks can target power management features, forcing devices into high-power states to drain batteries. Secure boot and authenticated firmware updates are crucial to prevent malicious power state manipulation.
