[CHUNK: DEFINITION]
Level of Detail (LOD) is a technique that reduces the complexity of 3D models or scenes based on their distance from the viewer or their perceived importance. Culling refers to methods that eliminate objects or parts of objects from the rendering pipeline that are not visible to the camera. Both are crucial for optimizing real-time graphics performance.

[CHUNK: CORE_CONCEPTS]
- Level of Detail (LOD): Reducing geometric complexity of objects based on distance or importance.
- View Frustum Culling: Discarding objects entirely outside the camera's view frustum.
- Occlusion Culling: Eliminating objects hidden behind other objects from the rendering process.
- Back-face Culling: Removing polygons facing away from the camera, typically for solid objects.
- Geometric LOD: Using simplified mesh versions for distant objects.
- Image-based LOD: Using 2D representations or imposters for very distant or complex objects.
- Hierarchical Culling: Organizing scene data in structures like octrees or k-d trees for efficient culling.
- Dynamic LOD: Adjusting LOD levels in real-time based on various criteria.

[CHUNK: SYSTEM_DESIGN_PERSPECTIVE]
LOD and culling are integrated early in the rendering pipeline to minimize data processed by subsequent stages. They often rely on spatial data structures (e.g., octrees, k-d trees) for efficient object management and visibility tests. Effective implementation requires balancing visual quality with performance gains, often involving asynchronous processing or GPU-accelerated techniques.

[CHUNK: REAL_WORLD_USE]
In video games, LOD ensures smooth frame rates by rendering detailed character models up close and simplified versions far away. Architectural visualization uses culling to prevent rendering rooms or buildings not currently in view. Flight simulators employ terrain LOD to manage vast landscapes efficiently, displaying high-detail textures and geometry only for nearby areas.
