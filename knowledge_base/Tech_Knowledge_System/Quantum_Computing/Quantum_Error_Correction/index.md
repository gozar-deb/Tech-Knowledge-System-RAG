## Quick Definition
Quantum Error Correction (QEC) is a vital set of techniques in quantum computing that protects delicate quantum information from errors caused by environmental noise and imperfect operations. It achieves this by encoding quantum states redundantly across multiple physical qubits, allowing for error detection and correction without collapsing the quantum state.

## Key Concepts
- Logical Qubits → Error-protected quantum bits formed by encoding information across several physical qubits.
- Physical Qubits → The actual, error-prone quantum bits that constitute quantum hardware.
- Decoherence → The process where quantum systems lose their quantum properties due to interaction with their environment.
- Quantum Noise → Random fluctuations or disturbances that corrupt quantum states, leading to computational errors.
- Stabilizer Codes → A class of quantum codes used to detect and correct errors by measuring multi-qubit operators.
- Fault Tolerance → The design principle ensuring quantum computations remain reliable despite errors in components.
- Syndrome Measurement → A technique to identify the type and location of errors without directly observing the quantum state.
