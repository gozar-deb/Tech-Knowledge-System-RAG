## Quick Definition
Superconducting qubits are quantum bits built from superconducting circuits that operate at ultra-low temperatures. They leverage quantum mechanical properties like superposition and entanglement to perform quantum computations, forming the basis of many leading quantum computing platforms.

## Key Concepts
- Superconductivity → Zero electrical resistance below a critical temperature.
- Josephson Junction → A quantum mechanical tunnel junction essential for qubit functionality.
- Artificial Atoms → Superconducting circuits engineered to behave like quantum two-level systems.
- Transmon Qubit → A robust type of superconducting qubit with reduced charge noise sensitivity.
- Coherence Time → The duration a qubit maintains its quantum state before environmental interaction.
- Quantum Entanglement → A state where qubits are linked, sharing a common fate regardless of distance.
- Quantum Superposition → A qubit's ability to exist in multiple states simultaneously.
- Cryogenics → The science of producing and maintaining very low temperatures, crucial for qubit operation.
