[CHUNK: DEFINITION]
Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) are cryptographic protocols enabling one party (the prover) to convince another party (the verifier) that a statement is true, without revealing any information beyond the veracity of the statement itself. This ensures privacy and data confidentiality while maintaining verifiability.

[CHUNK: CORE_CONCEPTS]
- Prover: The entity demonstrating knowledge of a secret without revealing it.
- Verifier: The entity confirming the prover's claim without learning the secret.
- Witness: The secret information known by the prover that makes the statement true.
- Soundness: Ensures a dishonest prover cannot convince the verifier of a false statement.
- Completeness: Guarantees an honest prover can always convince the verifier of a true statement.
- Zero-Knowledge: The verifier learns nothing about the witness beyond the statement's truth.
- Interactive ZKP: Requires multiple rounds of communication between prover and verifier.
- Non-Interactive ZKP (NIZKP): Proof generated once and verified by anyone, often using common reference strings.
- SNARKs (Succinct Non-interactive ARguments of Knowledge): Compact, fast-to-verify non-interactive proofs.
- STARKs (Scalable Transparent ARguments of Knowledge): Scalable, post-quantum secure non-interactive proofs without trusted setup.
