[CHUNK: DEFINITION]
Adaptive control enables robotic systems to adjust their parameters in real-time to compensate for uncertainties, disturbances, and changes in system dynamics or environment. Robust control designs ensure stable and predictable performance despite bounded model inaccuracies and external perturbations. Together, they provide high-performance trajectory tracking and operational reliability for complex robotic tasks.

[CHUNK: CORE_CONCEPTS]
- Adaptive Control: Control strategy that modifies controller parameters automatically to maintain desired performance in the presence of system uncertainties.
- Robust Control: Control design methodology that guarantees stability and performance for a range of system variations and external disturbances.
- Uncertainty Compensation: Mechanisms within adaptive and robust controllers to counteract unknown or varying system parameters and external forces.
- Trajectory Tracking: The ability of a robot to follow a predefined path or motion profile accurately, a key objective for these control methods.
- Disturbance Rejection: The capacity of a control system to minimize the impact of external or internal disturbances on system performance.
- Lyapunov Stability: A mathematical framework used to analyze the stability of dynamic systems, often employed in the design of adaptive and robust controllers.
- Model Reference Adaptive Control (MRAC): An adaptive control scheme where the system's output is made to follow the output of a reference model.
- Sliding Mode Control (SMC): A robust control technique known for its insensitivity to system uncertainties and external disturbances.

[CHUNK: SYSTEM_DESIGN_PERSPECTIVE]
In system design, adaptive and robust control are critical for deploying robots in dynamic, unstructured environments where precise models are unavailable or change over time. They allow for the creation of flexible and resilient robotic architectures that can handle unexpected events, ensuring operational continuity and safety. This reduces the need for constant manual recalibration and enhances autonomy.

[CHUNK: REAL_WORLD_USE]
Adaptive and robust control are implemented in industrial robot manipulators for tasks like precision assembly, where payload variations or tool wear can affect dynamics. They are also crucial in humanoid robotics for maintaining balance and executing complex movements on uneven terrain, and in autonomous vehicles for navigating unpredictable road conditions and interacting with dynamic environments.

[CHUNK: FAILURE_MODES]
Common failure modes include instability due to aggressive adaptation rates, poor performance from inadequate uncertainty bounds in robust designs, and computational delays in real-time parameter updates. Optimization strategies involve tuning controller gains using metaheuristics, employing learning-based methods for adaptive parameter updates, and integrating predictive control to anticipate disturbances. [CHUNK: SECURITY_IMPLICATIONS]
Security implications include potential vulnerabilities in adaptive algorithms that could be exploited to induce unstable or malicious robot behavior through manipulated sensor data or control signals. Robustness against cyber-physical attacks is paramount, requiring secure communication protocols and anomaly detection systems to safeguard control integrity.
