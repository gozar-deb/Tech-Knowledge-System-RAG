## Quick Definition
Enterprise blockchain refers to the application of blockchain technology within a business or organizational context, often involving private or permissioned networks. Unlike public blockchains, enterprise solutions prioritize scalability, privacy, and regulatory compliance, enabling secure and transparent data sharing among known participants. These systems are designed to streamline business processes, enhance supply chain management, and facilitate inter-organizational collaboration.

## Key Concepts
- Permissioned Blockchain → A blockchain network where participants must be authorized to join and participate, offering controlled access and enhanced privacy.
- Consensus Mechanisms → Algorithms like Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT) or Proof of Authority (PoA) used in enterprise settings to achieve agreement among network participants efficiently.
- Smart Contracts → Self-executing contracts with the terms of the agreement directly written into code, automating business logic and transactions on the blockchain.
- Privacy and Confidentiality → Features like private transactions or zero-knowledge proofs that ensure sensitive business data remains confidential while leveraging blockchain's integrity.
- Scalability → The ability of an enterprise blockchain network to handle a high volume of transactions and growing data without compromising performance.
- Interoperability → The capacity for different blockchain networks or traditional systems to communicate and exchange data seamlessly.
- Governance → The rules and processes that dictate how an enterprise blockchain network is managed, updated, and evolved by its participants.
