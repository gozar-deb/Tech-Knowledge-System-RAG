[CHUNK: DEFINITION]
Smart contracts are self-executing agreements with the terms of the agreement directly written into code. They run on a blockchain, making them immutable, transparent, and tamper-proof. These contracts automatically execute predefined actions when specific conditions are met, eliminating the need for intermediaries.

[CHUNK: CORE_CONCEPTS]
- Immutability: Once deployed, smart contract code cannot be altered, ensuring reliability and trust.
- Decentralization: Operated on a distributed network, removing central authority and single points of failure.
- Determinism: Given the same input, a smart contract will always produce the same output.
- Gas: A unit of computational effort required to execute operations on the Ethereum blockchain, paid in Ether.
- Oracles: External data feeds that provide real-world information to smart contracts, bridging the on-chain/off-chain gap.
- EVM (Ethereum Virtual Machine): The runtime environment for smart contracts on Ethereum, executing bytecode.

[CHUNK: SYSTEM_DESIGN_PERSPECTIVE]
Smart contracts integrate into distributed systems as autonomous agents, managing state transitions and asset transfers based on programmed logic. They form the backend logic for DApps, interacting with user interfaces and external services via oracles. Their design necessitates careful consideration of gas costs and execution limits.

[CHUNK: REAL_WORLD_USE]
Smart contracts power decentralized finance (DeFi) protocols for lending, borrowing, and automated market making. They are used in supply chain management for transparent tracking and automated payments. Furthermore, they enable the creation of NFTs and DAOs, facilitating digital ownership and governance.

[CHUNK: FAILURE_MODES]
Common failure modes include reentrancy attacks, integer overflows/underflows, and front-running vulnerabilities due to transaction ordering. Logic bugs in contract code can lead to irreversible loss of funds or unintended behavior, highlighting the critical need for rigorous auditing and testing.

[CHUNK: OPTIMIZATION_STRATEGIES]
Optimization involves minimizing gas consumption through efficient code design, state variable packing, and reducing external calls. Utilizing layer-2 scaling solutions and off-chain computation for complex tasks can also enhance performance and reduce transaction costs.

[CHUNK: SECURITY_IMPLICATIONS]
Smart contracts present significant attack surfaces due to their immutable nature and direct handling of digital assets. Vulnerabilities can lead to catastrophic financial losses. Defenses include formal verification, extensive unit and integration testing, security audits, and adherence to established security patterns.
