[CHUNK: DEFINITION]
Autoregressive language models predict the next token in a sequence based on all preceding tokens. They operate on a causal principle, where each output depends solely on past inputs, making them ideal for sequential data generation. This foundational approach underpins many state-of-the-art natural language processing tasks.

[CHUNK: CORE_CONCEPTS]
- Causal Masking: Prevents attention to future tokens during training, enforcing the autoregressive property.
- Next-Token Prediction: The primary objective, predicting the probability distribution of the next word or subword unit.
- Transformer Architecture: Dominant neural network architecture, utilizing self-attention mechanisms for context capture.
- Context Window: The fixed number of preceding tokens an attention mechanism can consider for prediction.
- Tokenization: Process of converting raw text into discrete units (tokens) for model input and output.
- Sampling Strategies: Methods like greedy, beam search, or top-k/nucleus sampling to generate diverse and coherent sequences.

[CHUNK: SYSTEM_DESIGN_PERSPECTIVE]
In system design, autoregressive models are typically deployed as inference engines, consuming input prompts and generating continuations. Their sequential nature often dictates batching strategies and hardware acceleration for efficient throughput. Scalability challenges arise from the quadratic complexity of attention mechanisms with sequence length.

[CHUNK: REAL_WORLD_USE]
Autoregressive models power conversational AI agents by generating human-like responses to user queries. They are integral to content creation platforms, assisting with drafting articles, emails, and creative writing. Furthermore, they enable code generation tools, translating natural language prompts into functional programming code.
