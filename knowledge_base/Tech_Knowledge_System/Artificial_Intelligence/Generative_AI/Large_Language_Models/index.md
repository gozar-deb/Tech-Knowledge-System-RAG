## Quick Definition
Large Language Models (LLMs) are advanced AI systems, primarily based on the Transformer architecture, trained on massive text datasets to comprehend, generate, and interact with human language. They leverage self-attention mechanisms to process context and produce coherent, contextually relevant outputs for a wide range of natural language tasks.

## Key Concepts
- Transformer Architecture → Foundation of most modern LLMs, enabling parallel processing of sequences.
- Self-Attention → Mechanism allowing LLMs to weigh the importance of different words in a sequence for context.
- Tokenization → Process of converting raw text into numerical tokens for model input.
- Pre-training → Initial phase of training on vast datasets to learn general language patterns.
- Fine-tuning → Adapting a pre-trained LLM to specific tasks or domains with specialized data.
- Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) → Architectural innovation increasing model capacity while maintaining computational efficiency by activating only a subset of experts per token.
