[CHUNK: DEFINITION]
Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) are a class of neural networks designed to process sequential data by maintaining an internal state or memory. Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks are a specialized type of RNN that mitigate the vanishing gradient problem, enabling them to learn and remember long-term dependencies in sequences through sophisticated gating mechanisms.

[CHUNK: CORE_CONCEPTS]
- Recurrent Neural Network (RNN): A neural network architecture adept at handling sequential data, characterized by connections that form directed cycles, allowing information to persist.
- Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM): An advanced RNN variant featuring a memory cell and three gates (input, forget, output) to control information flow, addressing the vanishing gradient issue.
- Vanishing Gradient Problem: A challenge in training deep neural networks, especially RNNs, where gradients shrink exponentially during backpropagation, preventing learning of long-term dependencies.
- Exploding Gradient Problem: An issue where gradients grow uncontrollably large during training, leading to unstable networks and large weight updates.
