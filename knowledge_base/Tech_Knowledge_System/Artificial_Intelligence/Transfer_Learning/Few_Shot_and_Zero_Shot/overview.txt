[CHUNK: DEFINITION]
Few-shot learning enables models to generalize to new tasks with minimal labeled examples, often by leveraging prior knowledge. Zero-shot learning extends this by allowing models to recognize unseen classes without any direct training examples, relying on auxiliary information like semantic descriptions. Both paradigms aim to improve data efficiency and adaptability in machine learning systems.

[CHUNK: CORE_CONCEPTS]
- Meta-Learning: Learning to learn, where a model is trained on a variety of tasks to acquire a generalizable learning strategy.
- Task Adaptation: Adjusting a pre-trained model to perform well on a new, related task with limited data.
- Prototype Networks: Models that classify by computing distances to class-specific prototypes in an embedding space.
- Metric Learning: Training models to learn an embedding space where similar examples are close and dissimilar ones are far apart.
- Inductive Bias: Assumptions made by a learning algorithm to generalize from limited training data to unseen examples.
- Semantic Embeddings: Representing words or concepts as vectors in a continuous space, capturing their meaning and relationships.
- Knowledge Graph: Structured representation of facts and relationships, used in zero-shot learning to link unseen classes to known attributes.
- Episode-based Training: A meta-learning training regime where each training step simulates a few-shot learning scenario.

[CHUNK: SYSTEM_DESIGN_PERSPECTIVE]
Integrating few-shot/zero-shot capabilities often involves a two-stage architecture: a meta-learner or pre-trained feature extractor, followed by a rapid adaptation module. This design prioritizes efficient knowledge transfer and generalization from abundant source data to scarce target data. The system must handle diverse task distributions and maintain performance under data scarcity.

[CHUNK: REAL_WORLD_USE]
Few-shot learning is applied in medical image diagnosis for rare diseases, where labeled data is scarce. Zero-shot learning is crucial for identifying new species in ecological monitoring or detecting emerging threats in cybersecurity without prior examples. It also powers content recommendation systems for novel items.

[CHUNK: FAILURE_MODES]
Common failures include overfitting to the few available examples, poor generalization to truly novel tasks, and reliance on brittle auxiliary information. Mismatched domain distributions between source and target tasks can severely degrade performance. Semantic gaps in zero-shot descriptions also lead to misclassification.

[CHUNK: OPTIMIZATION_STRATEGIES]
Optimizations involve robust meta-learning algorithms, sophisticated regularization techniques to prevent overfitting, and advanced data augmentation for low-resource settings. Ensemble methods and model distillation can enhance generalization. Careful selection and engineering of auxiliary information are critical for zero-shot performance.

[CHUNK: SECURITY_IMPLICATIONS]
Few-shot models can be vulnerable to adversarial attacks that exploit their sensitivity to small data perturbations, potentially leading to misclassification with minimal input changes. Data poisoning in the meta-training phase can compromise the model's ability to learn new tasks effectively. Robustness to noisy or malicious few-shot examples is a concern.
